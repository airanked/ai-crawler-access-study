AI Crawler Access Study

We fetched the robots.txt and llms.txt of 285 major websites across 10 categories and checked each file against 14 AI user agents, with Googlebot as a control. The dataset has one row per domain: whether the fetch worked, the allowed or blocked result for every agent at the homepage path, and whether the site serves a valid llms.txt. It is free to reuse with attribution.

  • 11.5%block OAI-SearchBot, the least blocked AI agent
  • 31 of 59GPTBot blockers still allow OAI-SearchBot
  • 88% vs 12%news vs B2B SaaS sites blocking a training bot
  • 81 of 241sites serve a valid llms.txt (34%); B2B SaaS leads at 64%

Method

  • Sample: 285 hand-picked domains in 10 categories, including 102 B2B SaaS companies and 53 news and media publishers. Each domain appears once.
  • Collection: October 2026, a Python script requested /robots.txt and /llms.txt on each domain with a 10-second timeout, redirects followed and one retry (including the www host).
  • Base: 24 domains that refused, timed out or returned an HTML page were excluded from every percentage, leaving n = 261 for robots.txt.
  • Parser: RFC 9309 rules evaluated at the path /: merged groups, fallback to the * group, longest match wins, Allow wins a tie, * and $ wildcards, case-insensitive agent names.
  • llms.txt: counted only on HTTP 200 with a text content type and a Markdown H1 first line; the adoption base is the 241 domains that returned 200, 404 or 410.
